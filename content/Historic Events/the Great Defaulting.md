### The Collapse of the Soul-Backed Economy
Historians who have managed to reconstruct the period trace the origin to the Incarnadine Exchange: a now-defunct financial institution operating out of Aundair that had, for decades, been issuing soul-promissory notes at a rate far exceeding the [[the Church of Arkanos|church's]] ability to underwrite them.

The logic had seemed sound at the time. Soul-pledges from living believers held genuine metaphysical value, verified by the church, endorsed by the weight of [[Arkanos]]'s infernal authority. A promise of a soul upon death, collateralised by ongoing tithe and devotion, was considered as close to a risk-free asset as the mortal world could produce.

What the Incarnadine Exchange and, it later emerged, several other regional High Debtors had done was issue promissory notes against souls that had never been pledged, projections of future converts, estimated devotional yield, speculative spiritual futures. The notes circulated, were traded, were used as collateral for further notes and entire mercantile economies ran on paper backed by faith backed by nothing.

The collapse began when a [[the Cobalt Soul|Cobalt Soul]] Expositor operating in Aundair delivered a sealed report to the High Curator of the Khorvaire Archive. The report contained a single devastating piece of information: the Incarnadine Exchange held soul-promissory notes totalling approximately forty thousand souls. Verified soul-pledges on record with the church in the entire region numbered fewer than seven thousand.

%% The High Curator sat on the report for eleven days. Whether this was due to disbelief, political calculation, or an attempt to quietly extract personal assets before publication remains a matter of historical controversy. On the twelfth day, the report was made public. %%

The effect was immediate and catastrophic. Merchants who had accepted soul-notes as payment called in their debts. Debtors who could not pay found their notes worthless. Credit, the entire architecture of trust that [[the Church of Arkanos]] had spent two centuries constructing, evaporated in a matter of weeks. Three major trading cities in the [[the Dwendalian Empire]] suspended commerce entirely for over a month with [[Tal'Dorei]]. Two noble houses collapsed. Fairhaven city council was dissolved by riot.

The church attempted to stabilise the situation by offering to honour all notes through a restructured devotional payment plan, essentially asking the public to simply tithe more to cover the shortfall. The proposal was not well received.

Within six months, the [[Tal’Dorei Council]] had formally condemned the church and begun seizing its assets. The [[the Dwendalian Empire]], never comfortable with an institution that rivalled its own financial authority, followed within the year. High Debtors were arrested, tried, and in most cases executed. Church properties were repurposed. The Master Ledger was never recovered.

The souls that were already pledged and already claimed was never officially addressed. Theologians of other faiths suggested that [[Arkanos]], as an arch-devil of some considerable power, may have simply kept what was contractually his.

%% Reports in the decades following the Defaulting described individuals afflicted with a peculiar spiritual hollowness: present, functional, but somehow less. Church survivors called this phenomenon the Foreclosed. Most governments preferred not to investigate further.%%

The Great Defaulting left scars that reshaped commerce across [[Exandria]] for generations. The word creditor became, in several regional dialects, a term of abuse. Children were told that if they borrowed what they could not repay, a man in a red coat would come to collect something they could not name but could not afford to lose.

It is rumoured that [[Asmodeus]] holds [[Arkanos]] in a red-collar prison in Dis until all owed souls are delivered by [[the Church of Arkanos]] with interest.
