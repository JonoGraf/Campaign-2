### Overview
Khalavoun was a Djinni of considerable standing, a lord of the upper winds, given to grand gestures, magnificent opinions, and a palace of sculpted cloud that he considered the finest structure in any of the elemental planes. He was proud without cruelty, imperious without malice.

Then came Lohmstir, he does not speak of how the binding happened. Whatever dwarven ritual trapped him inside the pick, he arrived into a weapon already old and already purposeful, already hungry for giant blood and goblinoid ruin.

Khalavoun experienced everything Lohmstir's wielders experienced. Every kill. Every campaign. The pick's compulsion to destroy was not his compulsion but he was present for all of it, a passenger with no door to exit through. Over time, the line between witness and participant blurred in ways he has never fully reckoned with.

### Personality
#### Surface
Imperious, theatrical, easily offended by informality. Refers to himself in the context of his former titles and the correct way to address a being of his station.

#### Underneath
Tired in a way he cannot name. Grateful for freedom with an intensity that embarrasses him. Drawn to stillness. Sometimes loses the thread of his own grandeur mid-sentence, as if something caught his attention elsewhere.

Visibly warmer, though he would deny this if pressed.

### Pact
Khalavoun framed the warlock pact as a reward, a generous Djinni bestowing power on the one who freed him. This framing is genuine, and also convenient. The truth is that Khalavoun is not entirely certain who he is outside of Lohmstir's context. The pact gives him purpose while he finds out.

- He wants the warlock to be genuinely excellent.  Khalavoun takes the quality of his chosen representative personally.
- He has a lingering, unspoken hope that the Dankil/Mightyhammer clan endures somewhere. He will not ask the warlock to find them. But he notices when dwarven history comes up in conversation.
- He wants to rebuild something of his former self, the palace of cloud, the standing among Djinni society, the freedom of open sky. The warlock's growing power is also his own restoration.

#### Communication
Khalavoun speaks through wind, his voice arrives as a current of air that seems to come from just above and behind the warlock's left ear. In enclosed spaces, papers and loose cloth shift faintly when he speaks. In open air, the warlock's cloak moves against the wind's direction. He cannot be heard by others unless he chooses it.

**Register & Cadence**
Formal without being stiff. He speaks in complete, well-constructed sentences. He dislikes contractions.

**Frequency**
He does not chatter. He speaks when he has something to say, or when the warlock addresses him directly. 

**On Being Summoned / Contacted**
The warlock can reach Khalavoun by speaking his name into open air. There is always a brief pause before he responds.

**When Pleased**
He does not praise effusively. Approval comes as a slight elevation in formality he speaks to the warlock as he might speak to a peer rather than a charge.

"Well done, WARLOCK. That was well done."

**When Displeased**
He goes quiet first. Then, when he speaks, he is very calm and very precise. He does not shout. He describes exactly what disappointed him and why, as if dictating a formal complaint to a record-keeper.

"You had the advantage. You chose sentiment over outcome. I will not catalogue the consequences as you are living them. We will not discuss this again."

**On the Subject of Lohmstir**
He will not discuss it directly. If the warlock asks about the pick, Khalavoun acknowledges the question, says something brief and technically true "It was a long time ago. It is over." and redirects. Pushing further produces silence. The wind around the warlock simply stops moving for a moment.
