### Who He Was
Tolan Veck was twenty-three years old, born in Westruun, recruited into the Cliffkeep Mountain regional guard eight months ago. He was not exceptional on paper — adequate scores, solid physical conditioning, no prior combat experience — but he had the quality that experienced guards value more than any of that: he showed up. Every shift, every patrol, every miserable pre-dawn muster in the mountain cold. He showed up and he did the work without needing to be asked twice.

Valkryth was assigned as his patrol partner four months ago. Standard practice — the mountain pass is not a solo posting, the guard rotation pairs experienced personnel with newer recruits for exactly the kind of mentorship the institution would never call mentorship because that would require acknowledging it has a culture. Valkryth would call it practical. Tolan had a longer stride than expected and learned quickly which rocks were loose.

They had been walking the same circuit together for sixteen weeks.

### His Death

The Hydra took him during the mine rescue.

He was there the whole time — working alongside the party, alongside Valkryth, shifting debris, bracing beams, doing his job. Somewhere in the chaos of the rescue — the dust, the wrong light from the Breach-touched creatures, the noise and movement and everyone's attention pulled in six directions at once — the Hydra fed. By the time the last trapped miner was helped out and people were filing back into daylight, Tolan Veck was already gone from every memory in the shaft.

Valkryth walked back down the mountain with the party that evening. He cannot remember a single step of the last four months of patrol. He filed his incident report as a solo patrol response, because he has always filed solo patrol reports, because as far as his memory is concerned he has always patrolled alone. He went to sleep that night in a barracks with a bed he doesn't notice and woke up the next morning with a restlessness he cannot name.

Harven was documenting the rescue as it happened. His hand was already moving when the Hydra fed. He drew Tolan because Tolan was there to be drawn — present, physical, real, working beside the party in the collapsing shaft. A few minutes either side and there would have been nothing left at all.

The sketch is not a clue that someone was erased. It is the last evidence that Tolan Veck was ever real.

### What Remains

- **Harven's sketch** — Tolan stands with the party in the mine rescue drawing, close enough to Valkryth that in the image they look like they know each other. They did. Valkryth just doesn't know that yet.
- **The patrol circuit** — mapped and supplied for two guards. Rest stops spaced for alternating watch shifts. Supply caches sized for a pair. Hendrik has noticed the discrepancy between the circuit design and the logged solo personnel. He has rechecked it six times. He has not resolved it.
- **Hendrik's roster** — four months of patrol logs showing Valkryth alone on a two-person circuit. Clean, consistent, wrong.
- **The guard barracks** — seven beds in a six-man room. One is Tolan's, made with the neatness of someone who took the instruction seriously. No one touches it. No one can say whose it is.
- **The armour rack** — one set of guard armour that fits no one currently stationed in Jorenn. Sized for a young man, broad in the shoulders, not yet filled out. It has been oiled within the past two months by someone who no longer exists.
- **The initials T.V.** — carved into the pickaxe handle at the mine entrance. The same initials scratched faintly into the grip of a spare spear in the guard rack. The same habit. The same hand.
- **Valkryth's patrol pace** — he walks the circuit faster than the route requires. He has always told himself this is efficiency. It is not. It is the unconscious pace of someone who spent four months matching a longer stride and has not yet recalibrated to walking alone.
- **The letter** — Tolan was writing home to Westruun. It is half-finished in the guard post's shared writing desk, tucked under a ledger. The handwriting is young and careful, the penmanship of someone who learned to write later than he should have and practised until it was good. It begins:

> _Mother, the pass is cold but I think I'm getting good at this. My partner says—_

It stops there.
