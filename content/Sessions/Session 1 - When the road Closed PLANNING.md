Setting: Jorenn Village

A small mining settlement nestled at the base of the Umbral Mountains.
- One main road once connected it to the lowlands.
- That road now ends in a towering wall of shadow - [[the Umbral Breach|the Breach]].
- The only way in or out is a narrow, dangerous mountain pass.

Jorenn feels:
- Economically strained
- Politically nervous
- Quiet in a way mining towns shouldn’t be

Dust hangs in the air.
Pickaxes ring in the distance.
And beyond town - an impossible wall of darkness stretches across the valley floor.

#### Preamble
Nine hundred years ago, your predecessors saved the world.

You know the stories. Everyone does. The heroes who brought down Orcus, Prince of Undeath. Who unmade Tiamat where she stood. Who held Mechanus together with mortal hands when the gears of reality began to slip. The [[the Triad|Triad]] witnessed it — Torm, Ilmater, Tyr — and when it was over, the gods looked at what mortals had accomplished and made a decision.

They left.

Not in defeat. Not in anger. By unanimous decree, the divine withdrew. Avatars dissolved. Planar gates collapsed. Prayers still rise — they simply no longer receive answer. The gods are watching. They are done intervening. What mortals could do, mortals would do. The Age of Absent Gods began on the day of the Divine Sundering, and it has not ended since.

The world did not end. It adapted. Faith survived — quieter, harder, built on conviction rather than miracle. Magic persisted — rarer now, less ambient, more deliberate. Civilisations rebuilt on mortal will alone and found, to their occasional surprise, that mortal will was sufficient.

But the Sundering left a scar.

No one fully understands it. The scholars of the Cobalt Soul have theories. The Ashari have older ones. What is agreed upon is this: when Orcus and Tiamat died, something structural died with them. Something that had been holding a tension in the fabric of the world released all at once — and the world tore.

The Umbral Breach opened in the Black Valley, north of Westruun, in the shadow of the Cliffkeep Mountains. Miles wide. A gulf of slow-moving darkness, like ink suspended in the air, where light bends wrong and sound dies and spells unravel at the edge. It does not stay still. It breathes. It narrows and widens without pattern or warning. Things thrown into it are never seen again — though sometimes, in the deep hours of the night, they are heard.

The Blackvalley Path is gone — swallowed in the first years after the Sundering, cutting off the eastern settlements from the rest of Tal'Dorei. The only way in or out now is the mountain pass through the Umbra Hills. Dangerous. Slow. Necessary.

At the edge of the Breach, in the shadow of hills that were dark long before the rift arrived, sits Jorenn Village.

It was a pioneer town once. Shadegrass and thin soil and people stubborn enough to call it home. Then silver was found in the mountain rock, and Jorenn became something — a boom town, a destination, a place worth the difficult journey through the pass. It is still that, technically. The mine still runs. The market still trades. The Tal'Dorei Council's authority still technically extends this far, though Jorenn has learned not to wait for it.

The unofficial account of Jorenn is harder to put into words. Locals will tell you the town feels right, if you ask them. Normal. Functional. Fine.

They will tell you this with the faint unease of people who have recently had to remind themselves it's true.

Six strangers made the journey through the Umbra Hills this week. Each for their own reasons. Each arriving at the same cold, silver-veined town at the edge of the world's longest wound.

**Valkryth** knows this road. He has walked it in both directions more times than he can count it is, after all, his road. The Cliffkeep Mountain regional guard does not have a glamorous posting, but it has this one, and Valkryth has made it his. The pass is in part his responsibility. The village at its end is his jurisdiction. He has protected this stretch of mountain by himself for long enough that the rocks know his footsteps. He is not arriving today so much as returning, which is its own kind of weight, in a town that has been feeling wrong in ways his incident reports cannot quite capture.

**Yoa** took the scenic route. That is, in fairness, the point the gap year was supposed to be scenic. Her family's business will be waiting when she returns, patient and inevitable as inheritance always is, and until then the world is large and full of things a Tabaxi with good reflexes and a flexible definition of employment can get into. Jorenn was not on any list she made. But a job is a job, a road is a road, and the mountains were interesting enough. She came through the pass three days ago and has been quietly deciding whether to leave ever since. Something keeps not quite letting her go.

**Sevrian Moonperal** has a quota. The Church of Arkanos does not use that word, they use _ministry targets_ and _outreach benchmarks_ — but Sevrian is a fairy with a good ear for language and knows a quota when it's handed to them in a sealed envelope with the Church's seal on it. Jorenn Village: population sufficient, silver economy, spiritually underserved, no current banking infrastructure. The Church considers this an opportunity. Sevrian considers it the edge of the known world. They have been here four days, have introduced themselves to approximately everyone, and have not yet decided whether the town's unease is a challenge or a warning. They are leaning toward challenge. They are also leaning toward leaving. They have not left.

The wind brought **Xyleria** here.
That is not a metaphor, or not entirely. The Djinni speaks in pressure and current, in the way air moves around obstacles and the particular quality of stillness before a storm. It has been guiding them for weeks  east, then north, then through the narrow cut of the mountain pass with something that felt almost like urgency in the way the gusts pushed at their back. They arrived in Jorenn yesterday. The wind has gone quiet since. Whether that means they have arrived somewhere important or simply somewhere the air does not move, they cannot yet tell. They are waiting to find out. The Djinni has not explained itself. It rarely does.

**[[Rik Banehammer]]]** came because of something he read.
Not scripture, there is less of that than there used to be, and what remains he has read enough times to have stopped finding new things in it. What he read was a surveyor's report, three years old, dry as chalk, filed in a Cobalt Soul archive he had no official reason to be in. It described the Breach's recent acceleration. The irregular shifts. The way the darkness moves as though something on the other side is pressing against it. And a footnote, added in different ink, in a hand that was not the surveyor's: _the membrane between here and elsewhere is thinnest at points of catastrophic divine failure. If the gods can be heard anywhere in this age, it would be here._
He does not know who wrote it. He does not know if it is true. He came anyway, because faith without evidence is the only kind left, and because a man who is looking for proof that the gods are still present goes where the gods supposedly bled.
He arrived this morning. The Breach was the first thing he saw from the top of the pass.
It did not feel like presence. It felt like absence, vast and patient and old.
He came down the mountain anyway.

**[[Zephir Theosore]]]** has a mandate. It is written on Arcana Pansophical letterhead, stamped with three seals, and uses the word _assessment_ eleven times in four paragraphs. The institution wants to know what the Breach is not philosophically, practically. Whether the magical degradation is mappable. Whether the planar thinning can be measured. Whether it is, in the language of the mandate, _a liability or an asset._
She has been here one day. She has filled six pages of observations. She has measured, catalogued, and noted everything the Breach presents to a careful eye at a safe distance.
What she has not yet written down is the way her notes from yesterday feel thinner than she remembers making them. That is not a datapoint she knows what to do with yet.
The [[the Arcana Pansophical]] expects a full report in thirty days. She suspects thirty days is going to be complicated.

Six strangers. One cold morning. One town that insists it is fine.
The Breach is visible to the west, if you care to look. Most people have stopped looking.
It doesn't seem to mind.
Tell me — what are you doing when the ground moves?

#### Convergence Event - [[the Umbral Breach|The Breach]] Shift

#### Setting the Scene

The preamble has landed. The players know where they are and why. Now place them:

Valkryth is coming down the mountain path into Jorenn, returning from a three-day patrol loop through the upper pass. He can see the village below — familiar, functional, his. The others are already in town, each occupying their own morning:

- **Yoa** is in the market square, having found nothing yet that constitutes a reason to leave
- **Sevrian** is introducing themselves to a merchant who does not appear to want banking services
- **Xylira** is standing at the western edge of town, facing the Breach, waiting for the wind to tell her something
- **Andrew** is at the village's small shrine — a stone alcove, no priest, the god's name worn off the plaque — trying to feel something
- **Eva** is at the well in the market square, making measurements with a piece of string and a compass, filling page seven of her field journal

This is where Oren is also visible — standing at the western edge of the market, facing the Breach. Still. Completely still. The market vendors have stopped noticing him. Xylira is twenty feet away from him and has been since she arrived. Neither has acknowledged the other.

Then the ground moves.

**The Tremor:**

It is not an earthquake. Earthquakes build. This arrives fully formed — a single deep shudder, like something enormous shifting its weight underground. Loose objects fall. A horse screams somewhere. The sound that accompanies it is wrong: a low harmonic that seems to come from inside the players' chests rather than from any direction.

**Individual character moments during the tremor — read or paraphrase as appropriate:**

_Yoa_ — the market stall she is standing next to sheds its awning. The vendor grabs her arm instinctively, then looks embarrassed. _"Sorry. You looked like someone I—"_ He stops. Lets go. Doesn't finish.

_Sevrian_ — the merchant they were speaking to grabs his wares and forgets Sevrian entirely, mid-sentence. Sevrian is left holding a pamphlet about Arkanos's Third Axiom and talking to no one.

_Xylira_ — the wind, which has been still since she arrived in Jorenn, moves. Once. A single deliberate gust from the direction of the Breach, carrying cold air and the faint smell of something that has no name. Her patron, communicating in the only language it uses.

_Andrew_ — at the shrine, the tremor dislodges a small stone from the alcove's back wall. It falls and reveals a hollow behind it — empty, whatever was stored there long since gone. The hollow is the exact size and shape of a reliquary. Someone removed what was inside. Andrew is left looking at an absence where something sacred used to be.

_Eva_ — her compass spins. Not wildly — a single slow rotation, settling fifteen degrees off magnetic north, pointing toward the Breach. She writes this down immediately. Her hand is steady. Her expression is not.

_Valkryth_ — on the path above town, the tremor is sharper. Loose shale cascades across the path behind him. He looks down at Jorenn and sees the Breach — visible from the elevation — has changed shape. A section that previously curved away from town now angles toward it. He has walked this path enough times to know that is new. He comes down the mountain faster.

**Perception DC 12:** The shadow at the Breach's edge moves against the wind for a moment before settling.

Within two minutes, a bell rings from the direction of the mine — three short strikes, repeated. A boy sprints through the market:

> _"Cave-in! Shaft Three! Foreman's calling all hands!"_

### PART TWO: THE MINE

#### Arriving at Shaft Three

The Silver Vein Mine is a ten-minute walk uphill. Valkryth intercepts the group on the way up — he has come down from the pass fast and is already moving toward the mine when the others pass him on the road. He doesn't need introducing to the situation. He needs introducing to the people responding to it.

By the time they arrive, **Foreman Brakka Stonevein** is at the entrance to Shaft Three, directing people with short precise gestures. She clocks the group immediately:

- Valkryth she knows. She gives him a nod that means _good, you're here._
- The others she assesses in under three seconds each. Her eyes linger on Sevrian — a fairy is not standard mine rescue personnel — but she hands them a pickaxe anyway. Small hands can still move rubble.

> _"You look able. Get in. Secondary collapse is possible, I need hands not questions."_

She hands pickaxes from the rack as people file in.

**There are seven pickaxes on the rack.**

She hands them out without counting. She does not notice the number.

**Investigation DC 10** to notice the surplus. If a player mentions it:

> Brakka stops. Looks at the rack. Counts — visibly — with her finger. _"Six,"_ she says. She counted six. The players counted seven. Move on.

**The extra pickaxe has the initials T.V. carved into the handle.**

**Character specific reactions to the pickaxe rack:**

_Eva_ — if she examines the rack: she counts twice, writes the number down, and underlines it. She does not know why she underlines it.

_Andrew_ — the initials T.V. catch his eye. He is the type to notice small personal markings on tools. Someone cared enough to claim this. Where are they?


#### Inside Shaft Three

The collapsed section is forty feet in — unstable but not catastrophic. Two miners are trapped behind a debris wall, audible and unharmed. The ceiling is cracked and occasionally dropping loose rock.

**Skill Challenge — Three Rounds:**

Each round, ask two or three players what they're doing and call for appropriate checks. DC 13 throughout. Failures produce complications, not disasters.

**Character-specific skill opportunities:**

_Valkryth_ — Athletics to shift the heaviest debris. He is the obvious choice and Brakka directs him there without discussion.

_Eva_ — Arcana or Investigation to read the collapse structurally. The cave-in has a pattern. She can identify which section to clear first without destabilizing the ceiling further. DC 13 Arcana. Success saves a round of work.

_Sevrian_ — Performance or Persuasion to keep a panicking civilian volunteer from making things worse. A young miner is frozen in the shaft entrance, blocking the workflow. Sevrian is, professionally, very good at managing difficult audiences.

_Xylira_ — she can use a cantrip to communicate with the trapped miners through the debris wall, establishing their exact position. Practical, efficient, slightly unnerving to Brakka who wasn't expecting eldritch assistance at a mine rescue.

_Andrew_ — Medicine through the debris gap to assess the trapped miners' condition. One of them has a head wound. Andrew can talk them through keeping pressure on it while the others work.

_Yoa_ — Acrobatics or Sleight of Hand to work in the narrow gaps other bodies can't reach, moving small but critical pieces of debris that are blocking the larger clear.

**Complications on failures:**

- A secondary crack opens — DC 11 DEX save or 1d6 bludgeoning
- A civilian volunteer freezes and blocks the work
- A support beam shifts, changing which debris is safe to move

**On success:** The two miners are helped out — shaken, physically fine. Brakka gives the group a single approving nod.

**The extra bedroll:** Perception DC 14 in the alcove where miners leave their gear. Seven bedrolls. One slightly dusty. A name scratched on the wall above it: _Tolan._ No last name.

If asked, Brakka says: _"Storage. Don't touch it."_ She doesn't know why she says that.


### PART THREE: THE CREATURES

#### What Comes Up From Below

As the last miner is helped out, something comes with them from the dark.

The tremor cracked a deeper passage connecting to a natural cave system under the Umbra Hills. What emerges was driven upward by the shift.

**The Encounter:** - 100exp each

- **4x Breach-Touched Darkmantles** — standard statblock, modified: their Darkness Aura emits cold colourless dim light rather than darkness. Everyone inside it looks like they're already dead.
- **2x Umbral Crawler** — Shadow statblock, modified: on a hit, DC 12 WIS save or the target cannot speak above a whisper until the start of their next turn. It feeds on sound, pressing toward anyone speaking loudly or casting spells with verbal components.
- S1 - 19 - dead
- S2 - 24
- D1 -9 - dead
- D2 - 14 - dead
- D3 - 8 -- dead
- D4 - 8 -- dead

**Character-specific combat moments:**

_Xylira_ — the Umbral Crawler targets her first. She is the loudest magical presence in the shaft. Her Djinni patron's influence reads as air and movement — the Crawler finds this interesting in a way that is not comforting.

_Sevrian_ — spells with verbal components trigger the Crawler's attention. A fairy bard who cannot sing is having a professionally terrible day.

_Andrew_ — divine magic hesitates slightly near the Breach-touched creatures. Not blocked — just a half-second slower than it should be. The first time Andrew casts, have him roll normally, but describe the light from his spell arriving _late_, like sound from a distant source.

_Eva_ — this is her first field encounter with Breach-tainted creatures. She is writing notes mid-combat. This is either admirable or deeply concerning depending on your perspective.

_Valkryth_ — has seen these before. Not often, but enough. He calls out the Crawler's sound-feeding behaviour to the group before it becomes obvious. This is worth something.

_Yoa_ — the Darkmantles' wrong light is disorienting for everyone but particularly for a ninja whose combat style relies on reading the environment. She adapts. She's good at adapting.

**The Umbral Crawler's end:** It does not die dramatically. It disperses — folds into a shadow that is briefly the wrong shape, then gone.

**Brakka, afterward:**

> _"This is the third time this month. Used to be once a year, maybe."_

She doesn't elaborate. She goes back to directing cleanup.

**Oren is visible at the mine entrance** during the fight — standing just outside the work crew perimeter, watching the Umbral Crawler specifically with an expression between reverence and academic interest. When the Crawler disperses, his eyes track the shadow. He mouths something — a held breath, released.

If approached: he produces a card. _I am a peaceful observer. I mean no harm._ He waits.

_Brakka, if she notices:_ _"Leave him. He's harmless. They're all over the place near the Breach."_ Already moving.

### PART FOUR: THE TOWN HALL

#### The Escort

**Captain Rul Hendrik** arrives at the mine entrance shortly after the creatures are dealt with. He already knows who everyone is — he keeps records of all arrivals. He thanks them by name. He gets Sevrian's name slightly wrong — close, but not right — and does not notice. He writes it down anyway.

> _"Reeve Morn requests your presence at the town hall. Standard procedure after an incident involving outsiders and a security event. Not optional, but I'm told there'll be food."_

On the walk, he asks each player a brief intake question and writes the answers in his ledger. When Yoa gives her name, something in Hendrik's pen-hand hesitates — barely, a fraction — before he writes it. He does not look up. He writes. He moves on.

If players ask why he keeps records:

> _"I like my numbers to be right."_

A beat.

> _"They haven't been, lately."_

**Passing the Ashveil Inn en route:** The claw marks on the left doorframe are visible to Yoa at eye level — parallel vertical grooves, deep, regular, the unmistakable work of a large felid maintaining their claws. Made habitually. Made recently. Made by someone who stood here every morning.

No one else notices them. Desh is visible through the window, wiping down the bar, her back to the door.

---

#### The Town Hall

A functional stone building in the village centre. Long table, mismatched chairs, a hearth working too hard, and maps of the Breach's recorded positions pinned to one wall — decades of entries in different hands, the most recent in a cramped tired script unlike the earlier ones.

Food: bread, hard cheese, a lamb stew better than it has any right to be.

**Harven Dell** is already in the corner, charcoal moving quietly across paper. He smiles when the group comes in. He begins sketching.

**Reeve Alric Morn** enters three minutes later. Mid-forties, looks sixty. Neat clothing, exhausted eyes. He shakes hands with each of them. When he reaches Andrew his grip is slightly firmer — a paladin is either very good news or very complicated news and Alric is not sure which yet.

He sits with a ledger he clearly doesn't enjoy carrying and gives the situation report:

> _"I'll be straightforward. The Breach shifted today by approximately forty yards toward the village. This is the largest single movement we've recorded in eleven years. It will likely correct. It usually does. The mine collapse is related — tremor destabilized Shaft Three's secondary supports — but the shaft itself is sound. We're not in immediate danger."_

The cadence of a man who has said this before and believes it less each time.

**Character-specific town hall moments:**

_Eva_ — Alric notices her field journal and asks, with controlled interest, who sent her. When she says Arcana Pansophical he is quiet for a moment. Then: _"Good. Someone should be measuring it properly."_ He means it. He also looks briefly, painfully relieved — the expression of someone who has been trying to do a job without the right tools for a very long time.

_Valkryth_ — Alric defers to him on the security situation at the mine entrance, asks about the pass conditions. This is the normal working relationship between a village reeve and his regional guard contact. It is also slightly off — Alric asks Valkryth about a guard rotation schedule, and Valkryth's answer doesn't quite match a roster Alric has in front of him. Neither of them can identify the discrepancy.

_Andrew_ — the worn shrine alcove is visible through a doorway off the main hall. Andrew can see it from where he sits. If he asks about it: _"We haven't had a permanent cleric in some years. Sister Voss tends it when she can."_ Alric says this without particular feeling. There is an unused office adjacent to the shrine. The door is closed.

_Sevrian_ — Alric is politely unreceptive to anything that sounds like a sales pitch. He has a village on the edge of a cosmic rift and limited bandwidth for the Third Axiom of Arkanos. However he is visibly intrigued by Sevrian's presence — a fairy is unusual in the Cliffkeep Mountains — and if Sevrian asks about local commerce his expression becomes complicated: _"We had a more active market, until recently. Things have been... quieter."_ He cannot say exactly when it changed.

_Xylira_ — Alric watches her with the careful neutrality of someone who has learned not to react visibly to things that unsettle him. A Drow warlock is not what he expected from today. If she asks about the wind near the Breach: _"It doesn't behave normally there. Nothing does."_ He says this with the weariness of someone who stopped finding it remarkable years ago.

_Yoa_ — when Alric is introduced to Yoa he extends his hand and then pauses. Something moves behind his eyes — the same reaching-toward-a-shape that happened with Desh. He covers it. _"I don't believe we've had a Tabaxi in Jorenn before."_ He sounds like he's reminding himself of something rather than stating a fact.

**Kaelin Voss** is present, sitting slightly apart. She watches the group with quiet attention. She gravitates toward Andrew instinctively then seems puzzled by her own impulse.

If anyone asks what she makes of the Breach, she touches her ring without looking at it:

> _"I've been counting the congregation. The numbers don't match the records. I thought I was miscounting. I've counted three times."_

A pause.

> _"I also keep finding myself humming a lullaby I don't know the words to. I grew up without siblings. I have no idea where I learned it."_

She does not connect these two things.

**The unused office:** Two coats hang behind Alric's chair — one his, one not. The second coat is slightly smaller, slightly more worn at the cuffs. If asked: _"Storage,"_ he says, and changes the subject.

**Oren — outside the window:** Perception DC 13 mid-meeting. He is in the street, at a respectful distance, watching the lit room with the stillness the players have now seen twice. If anyone goes outside he is gone before they reach the door — not fled, simply no longer present.

### PART FIVE: HARVEN'S SKETCH

#### The Closing Image

Harven has been in the corner the entire meeting. Near the end, as Alric is wrapping up, he tears a page from his sketchbook and holds it out.

> _"I try to get everyone who was there. For the record."_

**The sketch shows:**

- All six player characters, rendered accurately in charcoal — postures, equipment, faces
- Alric Morn
- Brakka Stonevein
- The two rescued miners
- A seventh figure, standing slightly to the left of the player group

The seventh figure is rendered with the same confident detail as everyone else. A young man, early twenties, solidly built, miner's posture. Looking slightly away from the viewer. Close enough to the party to appear to be with them.

**In the background of the sketch, rendered smaller but clearly intentional:** a figure standing at the mine entrance crowd. Still. Slightly apart. Watching. This is Oren — present, real, accountable.

The players have two anomalies. They will almost certainly decide they are connected. They are not.

**Running the moment:**

Don't announce it. Hand the sketch description to the players and let them find the seventh figure. Give them silence.

Harven is looking at it too. Frowning slightly — not alarmed. Puzzled the way someone is puzzled by a small technical error.

> _"Strange. I don't usually add figures I haven't... hmm."_

He tilts the sketch. Studies it.

> _"I must have been working from imagination. The composition needed balancing."_

He takes it back. Makes a small notation in the margin. Closes his book.


**End session.**
