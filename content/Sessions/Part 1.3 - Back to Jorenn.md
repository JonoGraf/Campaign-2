### PART FOUR: THE RETURN

#### Back to Davan

Davan listens to the account without interrupting.
> _"The Resonator is not ours. We didn't place it. If it was active, that means the Breach has been feeding it."_

He looks at it — or at the players, if they kept it.

> _"The Breach is getting louder. This is what that sounds like when it has something to vibrate against."_

He pays without complaint. If they kept the Resonator, Oren watches it with fixed attention as they leave. Davan does not ask for it. He does not need to.

#### The Cult Records

Handed over in a wrapped cloth bundle. Thirty years of Breach observations — dry, thorough, and deeply unsettling in aggregate. A player who spends an hour with them and makes DC 13 Investigation identifies the inflection point: three years ago, the Breach's behaviour changed. It began moving with more regularity. Less like a wound. More like something breathing.


### PART FIVE: HARVEN — THE CLOSING IMAGE
#### That Evening

The players are back in Jorenn. Desh puts food in front of them without being asked. Good food — she has decided she likes them. The fire is working. The Resonator, if they kept it, hums faintly when Valkryth walks past it. He moves to the other side of the table.

Harven finds them.

He looks different from session one. Not distressed — unmoored. He is carrying his sketchbook pressed against his chest with both hands. He sits without being invited. He opens to a new page and sets it on the table.

> _"I drew this today. This afternoon. I remember drawing it. I remember the light coming through my window, I remember the charcoal, I remember finishing it."_

A pause.

> _"I don't remember deciding to draw it."_

**The new sketch:**

The party — all six, rendered accurately. A street in Jorenn, near the market. An ordinary scene they were not present for today because they were at Ashmore.

![[Marketplace Drawing.png]]

> _"I think,"_ Harven says carefully, _"I think I know him."_

He touches the figure's face in the sketch. Doesn't press. Just touches.

> _"I can't remember from where."_

He closes the sketchbook. Stands. Gives them a small uncertain smile that doesn't reach his eyes.

> _"Probably nothing."_

He leaves.

### PART ONE: THE MORNING AFTER

#### Something Is Different

The players wake to Jorenn feeling fractionally worse than yesterday. Nothing dramatic. The kind of worse that accumulates.

**Three things they notice before breakfast:**

A merchant's cart has been sitting in the market square for four days. Fully loaded — bolts of wool, a crate of iron nails, two wheels of hard cheese. The horse is gone, stabled at the inn by someone who doesn't remember doing it. No one has claimed the cart. No one has reported a missing merchant. The market vendors work around it like it has always been there.

**Investigation DC 10** on the cart: a logbook is tucked under the driver's bench. The last entry reads: _Jorenn — delivery to the Voss stall._ The Voss stall — Jessa Voss's stall — is currently listed as unused storage.

Kaelin is sitting outside the inn when the players emerge. In the cold. Staring at her ring. She does not look like she slept.

> _"I conducted three funerals last month. I have records of all three. I remember two of them."_

She holds up her ring hand.

> _"I bought this in Westruun seven years ago. I remember the shop. I remember the price. I remember thinking it was too expensive."_

> _"I do not remember why I bought it."_

Harven's house, visible from the inn, has its curtains drawn. He is always up at dawn, working in natural light. This is new.

### PART TWO: THE INVESTIGATION

#### Thread One — Harven

The players find him at his kitchen table surrounded by sketches pulled from old sketchbooks, spread across every surface. He has been here since before dawn.

> _"I've been going through my work. Everything from the last year."_

> _"I draw what I see. It's what I do. I have drawn this kitchen, that window, this table. I have drawn the market, the mine entrance, the Breach from the hillside."_

He picks up one sketch — the kitchen.

> _"There is a chair in this drawing that I do not own."_

He sets it down. Picks up another — the market.

> _"There is a stall here that I cannot find."_

Another — a street scene.

> _"There is a child in this one."_

> _"I have been going through everything. I find them in almost every sketch from the last four months. Small things. Extra chairs. Extra coats. Extra people."_

> _"I think I have been drawing things I have forgotten I saw. I think my hand remembers what my mind doesn't."_

**What the sketches contain:**

- Multiple instances of a young woman tending a market stall — same face, never named, consistent across a dozen sketches
- A child, age approximately eight, appearing beside an adult male figure in several domestic scenes — the adult is Harven himself
- A young man in miner's clothing appearing in crowd scenes near the mine — consistently the same face
- In two sketches, a dark shape beneath a street-level grate, barely visible, like a suggestion of something large underground

**Investigation DC 12** on the underground shape sketches: Harven drew these on two separate occasions three weeks apart at the same location — the market square, near the well. He drew the shape both times without registering it consciously. His hand remembered it twice.

---

#### Thread Two — Hendrik's Records

Hendrik finds the players rather than the reverse. He is carrying his ledger and looks like a man who has been losing an argument with arithmetic for several days.

> _"I need an outside perspective. My numbers don't add up and I can't find the error."_

His record-keeping is immaculate — arrivals, departures, shift rotations, resource allocations. Everything dated, everything cross-referenced. And consistently, quietly wrong.

> _"Six weeks ago I had forty-three permanent residents and a rotating population of miners. I now have forty-three permanent residents. The number has not changed despite the fact that I have recorded three departures and no new arrivals in that period."_

> _"Food allocation for the barracks has not decreased. I am feeding the same number of people I was six weeks ago despite having, on paper, fewer of them."_

If players push him on what the third option might be — beyond wrong records or misallocated food:

> _"Something is consuming resources I'm not accounting for. Something present enough to consume and absent enough that I cannot find it in any headcount."_

He double-checks his ledger. Then checks it again.

**What his records contain for players who examine them:**

- Food consumption showing a consistent surplus — roughly one person's worth, every day, for six weeks
- A gap in the guard rotation — a name that appears in earlier entries, replaced by blank space in recent ones
- An entry from five weeks ago: _Greta H. — reports disturbance beneath market square, grinding/singing sound from below well. Investigated, nothing found._ Greta H. does not appear in any other record.

**Investigation DC 11:** Greta H. is not in the permanent resident list. She is not in the departure records. She arrived and then became a resource allocation anomaly.

#### Thread Three — The Well

The market square well is the oldest structure in Jorenn — predates the silver boom, possibly predates the village itself. Stone, wide-mouthed, capped with a wooden cover that is latched but not locked.

**Perception DC 10:** The wood of the cover is scratched on the underside — deep, irregular gouges made from below.

**Perception DC 13:** With an ear pressed to the cover, a faint sound from below. Not the Resonator's grinding pulse — something different. A low layered harmonic, almost melodic, that sits just below comfortable hearing. Intermittent. It starts and stops, like breathing.

**Investigation DC 14:** The well hasn't drawn clean water in approximately six weeks. Locals have been using the mine's water supply and have apparently not discussed why. When prompted, individual locals look mildly puzzled — _"the well? I suppose I haven't used it lately"_ — but cannot say when they stopped or why.

**The grate:** Six feet from the well, partially obscured by a market stall's awning, is an iron grate set into the street — a drainage access for the old below-street channel system. It is bolted shut from above. The bolts are bent slightly outward — iron stressed at the threads, pushed from below.

---

#### Thread Four — Petra

Finding Petra requires asking around about children in town. Most locals mention her in passing — _"deaf girl, lives with old Maret on the east side, quiet child."_

**Old Maret** opens the door with the wariness of someone who has watched her grandchild be dismissed repeatedly.

> _"She's been trying to show people something for two weeks. Nobody listens. Or they listen and then they just... forget what she showed them. They come back and ask her the same questions again."_

> _"She doesn't speak. She draws, though. Like that artist fellow. She and his girl used to draw together."_

**Petra** is sitting at the kitchen table with a piece of chalk and a dark slate. She looks at the players carefully when they enter — assessing, not afraid. She has been trying to communicate the same thing for two weeks. The moment she understands the players are actually listening, she begins.

**Players who engage patiently and make DC 10 Insight checks can follow her meaning.** A player who rushes or becomes frustrated will miss details.

**What Petra communicates, in sequence:**

_First:_ She draws two figures — a small one (herself) and a slightly larger one (Mira). Playing. She points to the larger figure and looks at the players expectantly.

If players show no recognition of Mira: Petra's expression shifts. She has seen this before. She erases the figure's face and redraws it more carefully. She holds the slate up. She waits.

_Second:_ She draws the well. The two of them near it — playing, as children do despite being told not to. She draws the cover being open — she mimes having lifted it.

_Third:_ She draws what was in the well.

A shape, below the waterline, enormous, with too many necks. She draws the mouths — multiple, open. She draws lines emanating from the mouths — sound, she mimes, vibration, pointing to her throat and shaking her head to indicate she cannot hear it but saw its effect. She draws Mira going still. Going glassy. Going away while still standing there.

_Fourth:_ She draws Mira walking toward the well. Climbing in. She draws the cover closing.

She puts the chalk down. She looks at the players.

Then she picks it up again and draws one more thing: herself, watching. Hiding. Not affected.

She taps her ear. Shakes her head. Points to the drawing of the creature. Points to her ear again.

**It couldn't reach her. She is the only witness because she is the only one the song couldn't touch.**

**Maret, from the doorway:**

> _"She's been trying to tell Harven. He talks to her, listens to her drawings, and then the next day he comes back and asks her the same questions. Every time."_

> _"She stopped going. It was upsetting her."_

**What players now know, assembled:**

- Something large lives beneath Jorenn, accessed via the well and the drainage system
- It produces a sound that affects memory and volition
- It has been feeding for at least six weeks
- It cannot affect those who cannot hear its song
- Harven's sketches show it twice — drawn unconsciously, same location
- People have been walking into the well willingly, without remembering doing so

### PART THREE: THE DESCENT

#### Going Under

The players have enough. They know approximately what they're dealing with. They know where the access point is. They do not yet have a plan for fighting something they'll forget the moment they look away.

They go down anyway.

**The grate** is the better entry than the well — wider, accessible, the bent bolts suggesting the creature uses the drainage tunnels to move. DC 12 Athletics to force it open, or DC 11 Thieves' Tools to unbolt it properly.

Give them one beat of preparation before they descend. Whatever they do — write notes, establish signals, prepare spells — let them do it. The attempt matters. It is also insufficient.

---

#### The Tunnels

The drainage system beneath Jorenn is old — pre-boom, possibly pre-village, carved channels of dark stone. Four feet wide, six feet high in most places, occasionally opening into larger junctions. They smell of wet stone and something else — organic, faintly sweet, the way something large and living smells in an enclosed space.

**The song begins immediately.**

A low harmonic that seems to come from the stone itself. Almost beautiful — resonant, complex, layered.

**The mechanic:**

From the moment the players enter the tunnels the Hydra's song is acting on them. Every time a player looks away from an area they have already observed — turning a corner, checking behind them, looking at a party member — the Hydra may have moved or changed without them knowing. Do not tell them this is happening. Just describe what they see when they look, which may be subtly different from what was there before.

The players' notes begin to fail them. If a player wrote _"large creature beneath the well"_ before descending, they can read those words. They understand them. They feel no particular urgency about them. The song doesn't erase the writing — it erases the _weight_ of the information.

**Do not tell the players this is happening to their characters.** Let them notice it themselves.

---

#### Three Tunnel Beats Before Contact

_Beat one:_ A junction chamber where four channels meet. The floor is covered in personal effects — boots, a mining helmet, a ring, a child's shoe, a guard's spear with the haft gnawed through. The possessions of the eaten, arranged with no logic. Not a nest. Just the debris of consumption.

**Perception DC 12** on the ring: it matches the description of the ring Kaelin wears on her left hand. Identical make, identical wear pattern. There are two of them.

_Beat two:_ The song gets richer. Fuller. A player with Performance proficiency or musical background notices it is harmonising with itself — multiple sources producing complementary tones. Multiple mouths. Multiple heads.

_Beat three:_ Something moves in the water of a flooded side channel. Large. Pale. Gone before it resolves into a shape.

---

#### Contact

One head emerges from the flooded channel. A neck like a pale tree trunk, a mouth that opens too wide, eyes that are filming white and do not track players individually but perceive the space they occupy as a whole.

**The False Hydra mechanic begins here:**

Players can perceive the head while looking directly at it. The moment any player looks away — to check on a party member, to reference a note, to watch their footing — that player loses all memory of the head and what it is. Not foggily. Completely. They are standing in a tunnel, slightly confused, vaguely aware something is wrong.

Run this for two or three rounds of chaos. Players who maintain eye contact are fighting something they can barely process. Players who look away are suddenly uncertain why everyone else is so agitated. Players who look back have to be told what they're looking at, disbelieve it for a moment, then lose it again the next time their eyes move.

**Do not let them win this fight.** This is not a winnable encounter at level 2 with no preparation. After two or three rounds:

A second head emerges from a different channel. Then a third sound from somewhere above — it is in the tunnels around them, not just ahead.

**The retreat:**

Someone needs to call it and make it stick. A player who has maintained eye contact the entire time is the only one with full situational awareness — and they are the one who has to drag confused party members toward the exit while not looking away from the creature.

DC 13 Athletics or Acrobatics to move fast through the tunnels while maintaining backward awareness. Failure: 1d8 bludgeoning from tunnel walls and stumbles, and one item from that player's pack is left behind in the tunnels — gone.

### PART FOUR: CRAWLING OUT

#### The Market Square, Dusk

They come up through the grate one by one. It is later than it should be — they have lost more time than the tunnels account for.

**What they have:**

- Wounds they cannot fully explain
- Dirt and tunnel-water on their clothes
- A pervasive feeling of having forgotten something important — like a word on the tip of the tongue that will not come

**What they do not have:**

- Any memory of the creature
- Any memory of the personal effects chamber, the ring, the child's shoe
- Any memory of retreating

**What they think happened:**  
They went down to investigate. Something was wrong. They came back up. The gap between these facts is smooth and featureless.

**Running the aftermath:**

Let them talk to each other. Let them compare literal notes, if they wrote any before descending. The notes say things like _"large creature, multiple heads, song affects memory."_ The words feel distant and theoretical — notes about someone else's problem.

One player may have written something on their own skin before descending. The writing is smeared from tunnel water but partially legible:

_DO NOT LOOK AWAY._

They don't know what it means. It is in their own handwriting.

---

#### Harven, Again

Harven is waiting at the inn. Petra told him the players went under — he doesn't know what that means but it frightened him enough to wait.

He looks at their state and does not ask what happened. He opens his sketchbook instead.

He has drawn again today without deciding to. This sketch is different.

It shows the market square from above — as if the artist were hovering at rooftop height. The players are visible, small figures, approaching the well. The seventh figure is with them.

But this time Harven has drawn what is beneath the square. Visible through the stone, rendered in charcoal with the detail of something his hand remembered clearly: a shape, vast and pale, coiled through the channels beneath the street. Multiple necks. Multiple heads. One of them pointed directly upward toward the figures on the surface.

The most detailed rendering of the False Hydra that exists. Drawn by a man who has never consciously seen it.

Harven looks at the sketch. He looks at the players.

> _"I don't know what this is."_

He tears the page out carefully and holds it out.

> _"But I think you need it more than I do."_

**End session.**

### WHAT THE PLAYERS NEED TO FIGURE OUT FOR SESSION 4

**The silence component:** Petra was immune because she cannot hear the song. Deafness — magical or physical — counters the memory effect. The Cult of the Open Silence becomes suddenly and urgently relevant.

**The memory anchor component:** Writing on skin worked, partially. It needs to be better — more permanent, more legible, more comprehensive. Whatever they choose, it needs to survive tunnel water and a fight.

**The looking away problem:** Even deafened, breaking line of sight still triggers memory loss. They need a system — mirrors, a rope order, a designated watcher whose only job is to never look away and keep calling out what they see.

**The Cult connection:** Can the Unvoiced suppress sound entirely? Can they teach the players the Vigil breathing that lets Listeners endure silence? This is the negotiation that makes the Cult a genuine uneasy ally.
