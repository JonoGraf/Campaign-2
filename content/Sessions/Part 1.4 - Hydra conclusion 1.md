#### Framing Device: The Council Report
The party leaves Jorenn Village with a mission to report the Umbral Breach to the Tal'Dorei Council in Emon. This is the **narrative spine** — loose enough that detours feel earned rather than derailing. The Council doesn't need to be a hard deadline; the urgency is real but the road is the story.

Two routes out. Whether this is a player choice or sequential is still open — the skeleton supports either.

#### Arc A — The Cliffkeep Trek

**Tone:** Survival, political tension, creeping dread at the margins

The Cliffkeep Mountains are scarred from the old Frost Giant invasion — ruined watchtowers, mass graves, abandoned dwarven outposts. The war is over, but a dissident clan, unwilling to accept the peace terms, has resumed raiding. Not a full invasion — something more dangerous: a clan with a grievance, a leader with something to prove, and no political leash.

**Key beats:**

- Travel through the aftermath of the invasion — environmental storytelling of what the war cost
- First contact with the dissident clan, potentially non-violent — they have their own logic
- A choice point: help broker something, fight through, or expose the clan to Council forces (with consequences either way)
- **Khalavoun hook potential** — the dwarven outposts in these mountains may connect to Lohmstir's history

**Open questions to develop later:**

- Who leads the dissident clan and what do they actually want?
- Is there a dwarven faction (Mightyhammer adjacent?) still holding a pass?


#### Arc B — Through the Breach

**Tone:** Cosmic horror transit → Gothic horror trap

**Part 1: The Road Train**

A magic-powered convoy — think armoured carriages on a Breachside rail or arcane-tethered chain — is the only reliable way to transit the Breach. It's a commercial operation run by desperate people. The party books passage.

The Breach outside is wrong in ways that are hard to articulate. Breach monsters attack in waves. The convoy has other passengers — some of whom are not what they seem.

**Part 2: The Domain of Dread — The Sundered Court**

Mid-transit, the convoy is pulled off-course into a pocket domain trapped within the Breach. This is your Strahd adaptation.

**The Darklord:** _Vael Mourne_ — adapted from Strahd, but reskinned to fit the setting. A warlord-turned-undead lord who ruled a small kingdom that was consumed by the Breach during the Divine Sundering. He believed he could master the dark energy of the Breach; instead it unmade his world and trapped him in a decaying echo of it, doomed to rule forever over subjects who rot and reset each dawn.

His curse mirrors Strahd's — **he cannot leave, and nothing he loves can survive him** — but his obsession isn't a reincarnated lover. It's _legitimacy_. He was never recognised as a true king. He wants a witness. Someone to finally declare his rule just.

**Key beats:**

- The convoy crashes into the domain — escape requires finding a way to "close" the pocket from inside
- The domain is a ghost-echo of a pre-Sundering kingdom, details slightly wrong
- Vael is not immediately hostile — he wants the party to _stay_, to be his court, to legitimise him
- The horror escalates as they realise the domain resets — NPCs don't remember, the dead return wrong, time is slipping
- Escape condition: something thematic — possibly forcing Vael to confront the truth of what he did to earn his curse

**Connections to wider lore:**

- The Breach and the Umbralic — is Vael's domain a preview of what the Umbralic wants to do to the whole Material Plane?
- Eva (Arcana Pansophical) may recognise fragments of pre-Sundering scholarship inside the domain


#### Landing Point: Emon & the Council

Whichever path they take, they arrive in Emon changed. The Council report is the scene — but the more interesting question is **what the Council does with the information**, and what the party wants from that interaction beyond duty. That's where Phase 3 hooks can seed.
