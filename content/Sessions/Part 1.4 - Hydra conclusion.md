### BEFORE YOU RUN: DM NOTES

**What this session does structurally:**  
This is the payoff session for everything seeded in sessions one through three. The players descend with a plan they built themselves, fight something they have to work to remember, and kill it. Then the world begins — slowly, painfully — to remember what it lost. It should feel like a victory that costs something.

**The plan the players arrive with:**  
You don't know exactly what they've decided between sessions — that's intentional. Whatever combination of silence, memory anchors, and line-of-sight systems they've built, honour it. The mechanic responds to creative solutions. If they've thought carefully, reward that. If they've missed something, let the tunnels teach them.

**The Umbralic seed:**  
The False Hydra has been feeding near the Breach for months. Something fed on it in return — drawing on its accumulated consumption, leaving a residue behind in its nest. This is not a second combat encounter. It is a discovery that reframes everything they just did and points toward the broader campaign. Handle it with restraint.

**Tone:**  
Sessions one through three built dread. Session four earns catharsis — but not clean catharsis. The victory is real. The cost is real. The thing in the nest at the end is a question, not an answer.

### PART ONE: THE DESCENT

#### The Grate — Market Square, Dawn

The party has chosen their moment. Dawn is correct — the market square is empty, the vendors not yet set up, the village still quiet. Whatever they're carrying — wax for their ears, written notes on their skin, a rope system, mirrors, whatever they decided — they have it now.

The grate bolts are still bent outward from their last visit. Someone has noticed and not reported it, or reported it and been forgotten. It opens the same way.

**Before they go down:**

Give the table a moment. This is the last breath of fresh air for a while. If any player wants to do something before the descent — check their notes, confirm the plan, say something to the others — let them.

Then the grate opens and the smell comes up first. Wet stone and something organic, faintly sweet. The same as before.

The difference: the song is already audible from the surface. Last time it started when they were inside. It has grown.

---

#### The Tunnels — Phase One

The drainage channels beneath Jorenn are familiar now — four feet wide, six feet high, dark stone, the occasional junction. But six weeks further into the Hydra's occupation, the tunnels have changed:

The walls are slick with something pale and translucent — not quite biological, not quite mineral, the Hydra's presence expressed as a slow secretion that catches torchlight and bends it wrong. The effect is subtle near the entrance and thickens as they go deeper.

**The song:** Continuous now. No pauses. The Cult records noted this — _harmonic output continuous for the first time. No pause. No breath._ The players are inside that observation.

**The memory mechanic — running it this session:**

The players have prepared. Honour their preparation:

- **Ear protection** (wax, magical silence, Cult breathing techniques): the song's memory-wipe effect is suppressed for that character. They retain full memory when looking away. They can still be frightened or unnerved by the Hydra — the preparation removes the mechanical effect, not the horror.
- **Memory anchors** (writing on skin, carved notes, external records): when a character without ear protection looks away and loses memory, they have something to read. The anchor restores the information but not the _weight_ of it — they know what they read, but feel no urgency about it for 1d4 rounds before the anchor reasserts the information's importance.
- **Line-of-sight systems** (designated watcher, mirrors, rope orders): a character maintaining indirect sight via mirror or description from a watcher retains memory as if they had direct sight. The designated watcher must never look away — if they do, the entire system fails for that round.

**What their preparation doesn't account for:** The Hydra has grown. It has multiple heads and uses them to attack sight-lines specifically — not because it understands the players' plan, but because heads that aren't feeding move to obscure and disorient. Their system will be stressed. Let it hold, mostly. Let it fail, once, in a way that matters.

---

#### The Personal Effects Chamber — Revisited

The junction where four channels meet. They have been here before and don't remember it.

The floor still holds the personal effects of the eaten. Boots, a mining helmet, a ring, a child's shoe, a gnawed spear haft. The players are seeing this for the first time consciously — their previous visit left no memory.

**Give them a moment here.**

Characters with ear protection see this clearly and have full emotional access to what it means. Characters without it see it clearly too — the song doesn't affect what they can perceive right now, only what they'll remember when they look away.

**Perception DC 11** — the ring on the floor matches the ring Kaelin wears. Identical make, identical wear pattern. There are two of them.

**Valkryth:** Among the effects, partially obscured under a boot, is a guard's belt buckle — standard Cliffkeep Mountain regional issue. Stamped with the guard corps insignia. Valkryth has one identical to it on his own belt. Issue items are assigned individually. This one has a name scratched into the back:

_T. Veck._

He doesn't know that name yet. He will.

---

#### The Tunnels — Phase Two: The Running Fight

The Hydra is aware of them now. Not intelligently — it has no strategy, no malice, no capacity for either. But it is a predator in its territory and the players have entered that territory making noise, carrying light, and moving with purpose. It responds.

**Structure:** Three tunnel encounters before the nest chamber, each escalating. The party is moving deeper — they can hear the song thickening, the walls getting slicker. They are going toward it. It knows.

---

**Encounter One — The Feeding Head**

A single head emerges from a flooded side channel ahead of the party, blocking the route forward.

This is the test of their preparation. Run it clean:

- Characters with ear protection: they see the head, they remember it when they look away, they can fight it with full coordination
- Characters without: the first time any of them looks away — to check a side tunnel, to pull a weapon, anything — they lose the head from memory. Their anchor tells them what they wrote. They have 1d4 rounds of feeling nothing particular about that information before it reasserts.

The head feeds by singing. It is not trying to kill them. It is trying to eat them — to lure them close, to make them passive, to walk them deeper while they forget why they came. Characters without ear protection who fail a DC 13 WIS save when the head sings feel a pull toward the flooded channel. Not compulsion — a suggestion, warm and patient. They can resist without a check. But they feel it.

**The head has AC 13, 22 HP.** When reduced to 0 HP it submerges. The channel is silent. Thirty seconds later, from deeper in the tunnels, a sound — the Hydra registering damage, adjusting, relocating.

---

**Encounter Two — Two Heads, Split Tunnel**

The tunnel forks. Both passages are viable routes to the nest. A head emerges from each simultaneously.

This is the stress test of their line-of-sight system. Two threats. Two directions. A designated watcher cannot cover both. Whatever system they built, it now has to make a choice.

**The tactical problem:**

- Split the party: each group faces one head with half the resources
- Stay together: engage one head while the other sings freely at exposed backs
- Find a third option: the tunnels have a junction point forty feet ahead where both passages meet — if they can push through one head fast enough, they can consolidate before the second head reaches them

**The heads here are more aggressive** — the Hydra has been hurt and is hunting rather than fishing. Each head: AC 13, 28 HP. They actively target the line-of-sight system — heads that aren't being engaged move to position between the watcher and the watched, physically blocking indirect sight lines.

**One failure moment:** Design this encounter so the line-of-sight system breaks once — a head interposes, a mirror gets knocked into the water, the watcher has to duck. One character loses memory for 1d4 rounds. Let them experience what session three was like, briefly, with the contrast of having been protected until now.

---

**Encounter Three — The Approach**

The tunnels open slightly as they near the nest chamber. The walls are thick with the pale secretion now — it catches their light and multiplies it wrong, filling the passage with a dim, directionless glow that makes shadows impossible to read.

No head appears. The song is very loud. They can feel it in their teeth.

**What they find instead:** The tunnel floor is covered in a thin layer of the secretion that has hardened slightly — and pressed into it, like fossils, are handprints. Human, dwarf, halfling. Dozens of them. People who walked this passage willingly, palms down, moving toward the nest. The consumed. Six weeks of meals, walking themselves to the table.

Among the handprints, if anyone looks carefully — **Perception DC 12** — a set that are distinctly not human. Larger, with the elongated digit structure of a felid. A Tabaxi's handprints, pressed deep, walking the same direction as the rest.

Clothesline came this way. Willingly. Not knowing.

**Give Yoa's player this without announcement.** Describe the handprints. Let them find the Tabaxi ones themselves.

### PART TWO: THE NEST

#### The Nest Chamber

The drainage system's largest junction — a circular space fifteen feet across where six channels converge, the ceiling domed eight feet above. It was built to handle the entire village's runoff. It has been repurposed.

The False Hydra fills it.

Not dramatically — not a monster bursting from the shadows. It is simply _there_, the way something is there when it has been somewhere long enough to become part of the architecture. Its body is pale, vast, soft-looking in the way of things that have never needed to be hard. Multiple necks emerge from a central mass that occupies the chamber's lowest point where the channels converge. The heads range the space — some submerged in the water, some resting against the walls, some moving slowly through the air above.

It does not look at them. It perceives them. There is a difference.

**The song is continuous and immediate.** Characters without ear protection are inside it completely — not louder than before, just closer, more personal, like it is specifically for them.

---

#### The Final Battle — Statblock and Mechanics

**The False Hydra**

_Large aberration, unaligned_

**AC** 14 (natural armour)  
**HP** 130  
**Speed** 20 ft., swim 40 ft.

|STR|DEX|CON|INT|WIS|CHA|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|18 (+4)|10 (+0)|16 (+3)|3 (-4)|12 (+1)|16 (+3)|

**Saving Throws** CON +6, WIS +4  
**Skills** Perception +4, Stealth +3  
**Condition Immunities** charmed, frightened  
**Senses** blindsight 60 ft., passive Perception 14

---

**Traits**

_Multiple Heads._ The Hydra has five heads. It can take one reaction per head rather than one per turn. When the Hydra takes 25 or more damage in a single turn, one head is destroyed. The Hydra dies when all heads are destroyed. A destroyed head cannot act or sing but its neck can still be used to physically block movement.

_Regeneration._ At the start of each of its turns the Hydra regrows any destroyed head unless it has taken fire damage since its last turn. A regrown head returns with 10 HP.

_The Song._ The Hydra's song is continuous while it lives. At the start of each round any character without ear protection makes a DC 13 WIS save. On a failure they lose memory of the Hydra and what it is until the start of their next turn — during which time they are not incapacitated but act on incomplete information. Their memory anchor restores the information but not the urgency for 1d4 rounds.

_Consume Memory._ When a character fails the WIS save by 5 or more, the Hydra's song pulls them — they must use their movement to take one step toward the nearest head. Not their action. Just their movement. They can choose not to use their movement that turn entirely to resist this.

---

**Actions**

_Multiattack._ The Hydra makes two bite attacks with two different heads.

_Bite._ Melee Weapon Attack: +6 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. Hit: 11 (2d6+4) piercing damage. If the target is a creature that has failed its Song save this turn, the bite also deals 7 (2d6) psychic damage.

_Overwhelming Chorus (Recharge 5-6)._ The Hydra sings with all active heads simultaneously. All creatures within 30 ft. without ear protection make a DC 15 WIS save. On a failure they lose memory of the Hydra until the start of their next turn AND must use their action on that turn to move toward the nearest head. On a success they are unaffected. Characters with ear protection are immune.

---

**Tactical Notes**

The Hydra does not fight cleverly. It has no tactics in the strategic sense. What it does is hunt — it uses heads not currently being attacked to reposition and sing, it targets isolated characters, it submerges damaged heads to protect them while other heads engage.

**Fire matters.** Players who have fire damage can prevent regeneration. Players who don't will find destroyed heads growing back. This is not a puzzle to solve mid-combat — it is a resource question. Do they have fire? Did they prepare for this? If not, the fight is harder and longer but still winnable. Reward preparation without punishing its absence absolutely.

**The line-of-sight system under pressure:** Heads actively interpose. The Hydra's five heads give it five potential blockers. The players' watcher system will be challenged every round — have heads physically move to break indirect sight lines. Make the players work to maintain what they built.

**The designated watcher:** Whoever has this role cannot attack. They are calling positions, describing the Hydra to characters who have lost memory, maintaining the system. This is a real sacrifice — one character's action economy removed from the fight. Acknowledge it. If the watcher does their job well, the party wins cleaner. If the watcher breaks — looks away, gets knocked prone, takes damage that forces them to react — run the chaos of session three again, briefly, before the system is restored.

---

**The Killing Blow**

When the last head is destroyed the Hydra's song stops mid-note. Not fading — stopping, the way a candle stops when you pinch it.

The silence that follows is the deepest thing the players have heard in Jorenn. The tunnels are suddenly just tunnels — wet stone, old drainage channels, the drip of water somewhere distant. Normal sounds. Correct sounds.

Characters without ear protection who remove their protection now hear nothing wrong. The song is gone.

The Hydra's body — the central mass, the necks, the heads — does not dissolve dramatically. It simply begins to deflate, slowly, like something whose structure was maintained by the song itself. Within minutes it is a pale, formless mass blocking the lowest point of the chamber. Within an hour, if anyone returns, it will be indistinguishable from the secretion on the walls.

### PART THREE: THE SEED

#### What's Underneath

The Hydra's mass, as it deflates, reveals the floor of the chamber beneath it. The drainage channels converge here at a central drain — a circular iron grate, old, original to the construction.

Through the grate, visible if anyone looks:

Not water. Not darkness. Something that looks like both — a slow movement, dark and slightly luminous, twelve feet below the drain. It is not reacting to the Hydra's death. It was not feeding the Hydra or directing it. It simply exists below it, the way water exists below ice — present, patient, prior.

It has no shape. It does not move toward them. It does not acknowledge them.

**WIS DC 12:** Looking at it produces a single sensation — not fear, not awe. Fullness. The feeling of a room with too many people in it, of air that has been breathed too many times. Pressure without direction.

**Arcana DC 15:** This is not a creature. It has no magical signature in any conventional sense. It is more like a property of the space — the way cold is a property of a room, not a thing in it.

**Eva's field journal:** If she records this, her description will be precise and will not quite capture it. She will cross out three attempts at classification and leave the entry unfinished. She has not left an entry unfinished before.

The grate is iron, set into stone, old. It is not locked. It is not trapped. Nothing about it suggests it should not be opened.

**If the players open it:** The sensation of fullness intensifies briefly, then subsides. Whatever is below does not rise. It does not respond. The channel below the grate is dark and still. After a moment it looks like it always looked — drainage infrastructure, nothing more.

Leave this unresolved. This is not a scene that explains itself. It is a door opening on a larger question. Move on.

### PART FOUR: COMING UP

#### The Market Square

They come up through the grate into afternoon light. The market square is occupied — vendors, locals, a child chasing a dog. Normal Jorenn sounds. Correct sounds.

Nothing has visibly changed. The memories will not return today. Partial return, gradual, over weeks — that is the nature of what the Hydra did and what its death undoes.

**What changes immediately:**

The abandoned merchant's cart — someone stops and looks at it properly for the first time in days. Not with recognition yet. Just with the sense that they should know whose it is and don't. They stand there for a moment, frowning, then walk away. The beginning of something.

Kaelin, if the players find her: she is sitting in the shrine alcove. She has taken off her ring and is holding it. She looks up when the players approach.

> _"Something changed."_

She doesn't know what. She puts the ring back on.

---

#### Valkryth — The Tolan Moment

This happens in the hours after the Hydra's death, as the first thin thread of memory begins returning. Not a flood — a thread. The first and most recent victims return first, faintly.

Valkryth is doing something ordinary — cleaning his weapon, updating his patrol log, eating — when it happens.

A face. A name. The specific weight of a pack that isn't his own being set down at a rest stop on the mountain path. The sound of a longer stride than his own on loose shale.

It does not arrive as grief immediately. It arrives as confusion — the confusion of a man whose memory has just told him something his records contradict. He has four months of solo patrol logs. He has four months of memory of walking alone.

He also has, suddenly and without explanation, four months of memory of not walking alone.

**Give the player space.** Don't narrate Valkryth's response. Don't tell the table what he feels. Ask the player what Valkryth does in the next few minutes — where he goes, whether he speaks to anyone, whether he checks his patrol log or puts it away.

What the player does with this moment is their own. What you provide is the detail:

The belt buckle from the personal effects chamber, if Valkryth took it. The name scratched into the back: _T. Veck._

The half-finished letter in the guard post writing desk, if anyone finds it now. _Mother, the pass is cold but I think I'm getting good at this. My partner says—_

Tolan Veck was twenty-three years old. He thought Valkryth was the best patrol partner he'd had.

---

#### The Closing Beat

That evening. The Ashveil Inn. Desh puts food in front of them without being asked — better food than usual. She has decided something about them that she hasn't put into words.

Harven comes in late.

He sits down at the table without being invited. He opens his sketchbook. He does not show them anything. He looks at a page — the party cannot see which one — for a long moment.

Then he closes it.

> _"I think I had a daughter."_

He says it the way someone says something they are not yet sure is true. Carefully. Holding it at a distance to check its weight.

> _"I can't— I almost— "_

He stops. Closes his eyes. Opens them.

> _"There are boots by my door. Small ones. I don't know why I haven't moved them."_

He stands up. He picks up his sketchbook. He leaves.

The boots are still there. They will be there for a while yet. Memory returns slowly. Some of it will not return at all — the Hydra fed long enough that certain details are simply gone, consumed completely, the texture of a person reduced to objects and residue and the ache of a shape that no longer has a name.

Mira Dell is not coming back. The memory of her will — partially, over weeks. The fact of her will not.

**End session.**
