### Backstory
![[Xylira.png]]
Your mother never spoke your father’s name.

Only that he had been a High Elf noble — wealthy enough to own servants, powerful enough to command a reputation in society, and selfish enough to let a Drow kitchen slave flee into the night carrying his bastard child. Whenever you pressed her for answers, your mother would retreat into silence, a tarnished silver pendant bracelet being the only personal possession you retain from that time. Some wounds, she warned, were safer left unopened.

<img src="../Assets/Xylira Hungry.png" style="float: left; margin-right: 20px;" width="300">
After running away from home, you grew up in alleys, market stalls, and crumbling tenements, drifting from city to city wherever coin, shelter, or pity could be found. Hunger became a familiar companion. So too did resentment. While noble families dined beneath chandeliers and studied in marble halls, you learned how long stale bread remained edible and which guards could be bribed to look the other way.

You naturally despise aristocrats — not merely for their cruelty, but for how easily the world bent around them. Somewhere among them lived the father who had abandoned you before you were even born. Though you had never met him, you often imagined the moment you finally would. Sometimes you pictured triumph, sometimes revenge. Most often, you imagined forcing him to look upon what he had discarded.

As a teenager, you became obsessed with magic. You believed that if you could master the arcane arts, you could carve yourself a place among the very elite who had rejected you. You spent years pursuing admission to wizarding academies, mage colleges, and private scholarly circles, only to find every door closed before you.

Some claimed you lacked talent. Others dismissed you as too old to begin proper training. A few simply looked at your dark skin and concealed their contempt behind polite smiles.
Still, you refused to abandon the dream. If discipline and talent would not earn you power, then you would simply find another way.

You began associating with vagabonds, smugglers, confidence tricksters, and petty criminals — people who understood that rules were obstacles designed by the powerful to keep others beneath them. From them, you learned how to weave your way into forbidden rooms, forge documents, disappear into crowds, and talk your way out of trouble with half-truths and confident lies.

By day, you travelled between academies, monasteries, and libraries performing secretarial work, copying manuscripts, and organising archives in exchange for room and board. Occasionally, you forged letters of recommendation or false admission papers for desperate apprentices willing to pay.

<img src="../Assets/Xylira stealing.png" style="float: right; margin-left: 20px;" width="300">
By night, in the comfort of the pale moonlight, you robbed the very institutions that had rejected you. You prowled silent corridors beneath candlelight, slipping through archives and forbidden storehouses in search of magical curiosities to steal and sell upon the black market. Though you lacked the education to understand many of the relics you encountered, that rarely stopped you. More often than not, you simply took whatever looked valuable, ancient, or dangerous.

It was reckless work. Time after time, you triggered wards, disturbed magical sensors and narrowly escaped summoned guardians. On more than one occasion, you avoided imprisonment only by planting evidence among another’s belongings or convincing your accusers you were a mere pawn in the game of an opposing House, letting the chaos of the political conflict distract them from your guilt.

Somehow, against all reason, fortune seemed to favour you, so you never changed your ways; although you occasionally gave alms to the poor, especially street urchins in exchange for insider information.

Such a tip led you to infiltrate the estate of a renowned collector of magical artifacts, posing as a culinary assistant. One dark evening, you snuck into a secluded windowless trophy chamber hidden down a dark hallway. Ancient weapons lined the room: ceremonial blades, cracked shields, spears blackened with age.

Your attention settled briefly upon a gem-studded kris resting atop a velvet stand. Then you heard the whisper – soft, calm, almost amused.

“Here.”

You froze. The voice did not echo through the chamber so much as curl gently through your thoughts like drifting smoke. Turning slowly, your eyes settled upon a cold iron-bound chest hidden in the corner of the room. The closer you stepped toward it, the louder the whisper became.

“Closer.”

The chest itself was immaculate, its surface carved with unfamiliar glyphs that shimmered faintly beneath your fingertips. Yet the padlock fastening it shut appeared strangely corroded, as though decades older than the chest it protected.

Every instinct warned you to leave it alone. Which only made you want it more. Using the kris as leverage, you pried at the weakened lock until the rusted metal crumbled apart like dried leaves. Inside the chest rested a large object wrapped tightly in layers of stained cloth. Laid carefully atop it was a single note:

"Restricted: Dangerous Artifact. DO NOT TOUCH OR ATTUNE!"

You smirked. For a fleeting moment, visions of gold flooded your mind. Rare artifacts fetched fortunes beyond imagination. Enough wealth could buy status. Influence. Freedom.

Eagerly, you cast the warning aside and unwrapped the bundle. Your excitement vanished instantly as you beheld nothing more than a rusted war pick.

Its metal head was dull and speckled with dirt; the wooden haft was worn with age, although you carved into its surface you observed crude Dwarvish runes, the thought of which made your stomach tighten with instinctive unease.

As you ran your fingers along the markings, you felt a breeze stir your silver hair. Startled, you stumbled backward as the pick floated upwards and hung motionless in the air before you.

Your breath caught in your throat as pale blue light slowly spread across the rusted metal. The whisper returned one final time, clearer now than ever before.

“Finally…”

<img src="Xylira freeing khalavoun.png" style="display: block; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto; text-align: center;" width="80%">

A violent chill raced down your spine, and perspiration evaporated at the cool touch of your bracelet against your skin. The blue glow vanished instantly, and the pick crashed against the stone floor with a deafening clang. Then came the shouts - the guards!

Boots thundered through the corridor beyond the chamber as panic seized your body. Without thinking, and still clutching the dagger, fled through a rear passage into the freezing night, abandoning your possessions, your lodgings, and the life you had known.

Yet even as fear clawed at your chest, another sensation pulled at you beneath it — subtle, impossible, undeniable. A presence - not commanding, not threatening; but guiding. Almost familial, the wind itself seemed to bend around your path, carrying you onward through the night until exhaustion finally overcame you.

You awoke the following morning beneath the sprawling roots of an ancient oak tree, wrapped tightly in your travel cloak. Frost clung to the grass around you. A croaky, high-pitched voice chirped nearby.

“Morning, moonshine. My master would like a word.”
