### Backstory
![[Rick Banehammer.png]]
Thorrik Banehammer was born in the shadow of the forge, raised among dwarves who believed that evil was simple: monsters in caves, raiders at the gates, demons in dark places. Hammer bad things. Protect good folk. Easy. Or so Rik thought. From a young age, Rik idolized the shining warriors of the Order of the Gauntlet. He dreamed of becoming a holy knight — a hammer of justice, defender of the weak, scourge of darkness. He practiced speeches in the mirror about righteousness and once tried to bless the family goat because “all noble companions deserve divine protection.” Against expectations, Rik was accepted as an initiate. He trained hard. Very hard. No one could question his courage, devotion, or willingness to throw himself into danger for others. He memorized scripture, learned martial discipline, and prayed with genuine conviction. There was just one problem. Rik was catastrophically bad at understanding degrees of wrongdoing.

To Rik, morality existed in only two categories: 
1. Good and righteous 
2. Probably secretly evil

The problems began small. A stable hand caught exaggerating prices? Rik loudly declared him “a merchant of corruption” and attempted a dramatic public repentance. A child caught stealing pastries? Rik organized an extremely serious redemption ceremony involving prayers, moral lessons, and an accidental lecture that lasted longer than the crime itself. A recruit sneaking ale after curfew? Rik once barricaded the dormitory because he believed “moral decay spreads faster than plague.”

The breaking point came during a mission to investigate reports of criminal activity in a nearby village. Rik became utterly convinced the local baker was an agent of darkness because he “had the eyes of a scoundrel” and occasionally overcharged travelers. The baker’s true crime turned out to be tax avoidance. Unfortunately, Rik had already rallied half the village, confiscated seventeen pies as “evidence,” and delivered an impassioned speech on corruption while accidentally setting a market cart ablaze. 

The Order quietly described the incident as “an overextension of zeal.” Rik was not expelled for cruelty — quite the opposite. The elders agreed his heart was pure. He was brave, loyal, and genuinely wanted to protect innocent people. But a knight of the Gauntlet required wisdom as much as conviction. One veteran paladin gave him words Rik still repeats to himself: “Boy, evil wears many faces. But so does foolishness, poor judgment, and plain stupidity. Learn the difference before ye swing the hammer.” 

Rik was dismissed from formal training — not in disgrace, but in disappointment. He was told to travel the world, gain perspective, and perhaps one day return wiser than he left. Rik took this as a sacred trial. Now he wanders the realm in simple, battered armor with more determination than experience, desperate to prove himself worthy of the Order. He tries earnestly to help people, even if his instincts are occasionally… misplaced. He still mistakes mild wrongdoing for grand villainy. He once spent an entire week investigating a tavern owner for “criminal secrecy,” only to discover the man simply hated conversation. 

Rik now keeps a leather notebook titled “How to Tell Evil Apart from Idiocy”, though its usefulness is questionable. Inside are observations such as:
* “Suspicious mustache = possible evil”
* “People who avoid eye contact = investigate further”
* “Tax collectors = uncertain”
* “Children stealing food = hungry, not evil (important lesson)” 
 
Despite everything, Rik Banehammer remains deeply sincere. He wants to be a true protector. And though he has stumbled more than once, there are moments — brief moments — where people catch a glimpse of the paladin he might someday become. And if someone truly evil appears? Rick Banehammer swings first and asks righteous questions later.
