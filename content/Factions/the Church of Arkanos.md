### Founding
The Church of Arkanos emerged in the centuries following [[the Divine Sundering]], taking root in the mercantile cities of the [[Menagerie Coast]] and the trade corridors of eastern [[Tal'Dorei]]. Its founder and divine patron, [[Arkanos]], is believed to have ascended to arch-devil status through mortal ambition alone. A former merchant-prince who bargained, leveraged, and outmanoeuvred his way into godhood. Whether this origin is literally true or a founding myth cultivated by the church itself is a matter of some scholarly debate.

In its early years the church offered something genuinely revolutionary: a standardised system of value inter-continental across all of [[Exandria]]. Merchants who tithed to [[Arkanos]] received certified letters of credit, backed by the church's spiritual authority and, theoretically, the weight of souls pledged in devotion. Trade became faster, safer, and more profitable. Cities that welcomed Arkanos's clergy grew wealthy. Cities that didn't began to feel the disadvantage.

At its peak, the Church of Arkanos operated temples in over eighty cities across Exandria, maintained its own courier network, and held more liquid wealth than several national treasuries. High Debtors sat at the tables of kings. The faith was not merely tolerated, it was infrastructural.

Then came [[the Great Defaulting]].
In the aftermath, the church was formally condemned across most of [[Tal'Dorei]] and [[the Dwendalian Empire]]. Its temples were seized, repurposed, or demolished. Its clergy scattered. Within a generation, lending money outside of the official financial systems had become illegal in many regions but also morally repugnant. The Church of Arkanos never recovered its former standing.
Officially, it no longer exists.
Unofficially, it never truly dissolved.

### Culture
#### Theology
The church teaches that value is the only honest measure of a soul. Not virtue, not lineage, not divine favour. What you produce, what you accumulate, what others are willing to exchange for your presence and your promises. In Arkanos's theology, the soul itself is a commodity: the most fundamental unit of worth in the universe, infinitely tradeable and, crucially, infinitely leverageable.

Core tenets of the faith include:
**The First Axiom - Value is Truth.** All things have a price. To pretend otherwise is sentimentality, and sentimentality is the enemy of clarity. A person who knows what they are worth is free. A person who does not is merely owned by someone who does.

**The Second Axiom - Debt is Sacred.** A promise of future value is itself a form of value. To make a debt is to make a covenant. To break one is the only true sin in Arkanos's eyes - not because of the loss it causes, but because it is a lie told in the language of numbers.

**The Third Axiom - The Worthy Rise.** [[Arkanos]] does not promise equality. He promises opportunity. Those with the intelligence, discipline, and will to accumulate will be rewarded in this life and elevated in the next. The poor are not sinful - merely under utilised.

**The Fourth Axiom - The Soul Matures with Interest.** Devotion to Arkanos is an investment. The more you give to the faith - in tithe, in service, in the pledging of your soul - the greater your spiritual return upon death. True believers do not fear damnation. They have already negotiated better terms.

#### Ideals
The Church of Arkanos attracts those who find meaning in systems, hierarchies, and measurable outcomes. Its followers tend toward lawful alignments not out of moral conviction but because reliable systems produce better returns.

Chaos is bad for business.

The church holds the following values above all:
- **Contracts over compassion.** An agreement honoured is worth more than a kindness given freely.
- **Information as currency.** Knowing what others do not is the most reliable form of wealth.
- **Patience as virtue.** The faithful of Arkanos play long games. Short-term loss for long-term gain is not sacrifice, it is strategy.
- **Accumulation without apology.** Wanting more is not greed. It is aspiration, and aspiration is holy.

#### Recruitment & Training
The church does not proselytise in any conventional sense. It does not stand in town squares or distribute scripture. Instead it recruits through need.

A merchant drowning in debt finds a quiet creditor who asks only for a signature and a small ongoing tithe. A desperate noble discovers a solicitor who can make certain problems disappear, for a fee and a favour to be named later. A talented young accountant in a struggling city is offered an apprenticeship with unusually good terms.

By the time a recruit understands the nature of the organisation they have joined, they are already indebted to it, spiritually, financially, or both. This is not considered manipulation by the church. It is considered good onboarding.

Those who show talent, loyalty, and an aptitude for ambiguity are elevated to Scribe status. Those who show exceptional intelligence and a willingness to operate in moral grey areas are assessed for Assessor candidacy. Advancement always comes with new privileges and new obligations in equal measure.

#### Hierarchy
The church's structure mirrors a financial institution more than a conventional religious order.

**The Sovereign Ledger** - The theoretical head of the church, believed to be an immortal or near-immortal figure who personally holds the Master Ledger: a bound tome said to contain the soul-pledges of every true believer who ever lived. Whether the Sovereign Ledger still exists as a living person, has become something else entirely, or is merely a myth used to imply institutional continuity is unknown.

**High Debtors** - The senior clergy, each responsible for a region or major operation. Before [[the Great Defaulting]], High Debtors were publicly known figures of considerable influence. Now they operate under secular identities, typically as merchants, bankers, or legal advocates.

**Assessors** - Mid-ranking clergy who evaluate new members, manage local operations, and appraise the spiritual and material worth of those who seek deeper involvement with the church. An Assessor's judgment determines what tier of the faith you belong to and what you are owed in return.

**Scribes of the Ledger** - The working clergy. Administrators, couriers, accountants, and enforcers. Most Scribes know they serve [[Arkanos]] but have limited knowledge of the church's broader operations. They are compartmentalised by design and may still only deal with the regional currency trade.

**The Unmarked** - Worshippers and those in the earliest stages of devotion. They may not even know the name of the organisation they are tithing to, recruited through front businesses and financial services that carry no obvious religious branding.

#### Uniforms & Equipment
The red coat of [[Arkanos]] is a staple amongst followers

#### Rituals
**The Tithing of Worth** The foundational rite of the faith. Performed upon entry, a new member makes a formal declaration of their current material and spiritual worth before an Assessor, who records it in a Ledger. This is not merely symbolic. It establishes the baseline value against which the church will measure your growth and your debt.

**The Compounding** An annual renewal ceremony in which members formally reaffirm their soul-pledge and receive a spiritual dividend in return, usually manifesting as a blessing of fortune, clarity, or persuasion depending on the cleric administering it. True believers hold that missing a Compounding causes spiritual depreciation.

**The Binding Contract** A major rite for those ascending to Assessor rank or above. A contract is signed in the member's own blood and filed in the regional Ledger. The terms vary but always include a clause surrendering the soul to [[Arkanos]] upon death with no possibility of resurrection without the church's explicit permission. This clause is not metaphorical.

**The Audit** A disciplinary and investigative rite. When a member is suspected of breaking covenant, lying about their worth, defaulting on obligations to the church, or attempting to leave, an Assessor convenes an Audit. The accused must submit to a magical examination of their Ledger entries. Those found in breach are spiritually written down, a process said to literally diminish the soul, leaving the subject noticeably diminished in charisma, confidence, and drive. Severe cases result in full _foreclosure_.
